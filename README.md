# Coptic Hymns Centre

**Coptic Hymns Centre (CHC)** is a unified Coptic Orthodox worship, Scripture, hymn, and liturgical resource platform.

CHC is designed to bring the texts, rites, hymns, readings, and recordings used in the life of the Coptic Orthodox Church into one modern application. Instead of relying on separate apps, PDFs, websites, and audio libraries, CHC brings these resources together in a consistent, searchable, multilingual experience for personal use, church services, study, and teaching.

The project is built with a strong focus on preserving the structure and tradition of Coptic worship while making its resources easier to access on modern devices.

---

## What CHC Is

CHC is more than a hymn library.

The app combines several major parts of Coptic Orthodox life into one platform:

- **Liturgical books**
- **The Holy Bible**
- **Coptic hymns and responses**
- **Psalmody and praises**
- **Saints and the Synaxarion**
- **Church seasons, feasts, and fasts**
- **Audio recordings**
- **Multilingual liturgical texts**
- **Presentation and slideshow tools for church use**

The goal is to create a single reliable place for the texts and media used throughout the Coptic liturgical year.

## Core Features

### Liturgical Books

CHC contains structured digital versions of the books used in Coptic Orthodox worship.

Rather than presenting services as static PDFs, the app stores liturgical material as organized content that can respond to the current service, season, feast, day, and context.

This allows CHC to present the appropriate prayers, hymns, responses, and readings while keeping the service easy to navigate.

Content can include areas such as:

- The Divine Liturgy
- Vespers
- Matins
- Midnight Praises
- Psalmody
- Doxologies
- Verses of the Cymbals
- Gospel Responses
- Praxis Responses
- Hymns of the Intercessions
- Veneration
- Seasonal and feast-specific hymns
- Other liturgical books and services

## Intelligent Seasonal Content

One of CHC's central goals is to understand the structure of the Coptic liturgical year.

Hymns and responses can be associated with conditions such as:

- Coptic season
- Feast or fast
- Day of the week
- Adam or Vatos days
- Weekdays or weekends
- Vespers or Midnight Praises
- Specific services
- Special liturgical occasions

This allows the app to determine which content belongs in a particular service instead of forcing users to manually search through every possible variation.

Examples include seasons and occasions such as:

- Nayrouz
- Feast of the Cross
- Nativity Fast
- Kiahk
- Nativity
- Theophany
- Great Lent
- Palm Sunday
- Holy Week
- Resurrection
- Pentecost
- Apostles' Fast
- St. Mary's Fast
- Major feasts of the Lord
- Other Coptic feasts and commemorations

The user-facing experience keeps formal feast and season names clear while the underlying system maintains consistent conditions for liturgical logic.

## The Holy Bible

CHC includes a structured Bible designed specifically for the textual needs of the Coptic Orthodox tradition.

The Bible database supports multiple textual traditions and languages while accounting for differences in chapter order, verse numbering, introductions, and Septuagint structure.

Depending on the book and language, CHC can include:

- **Greek Septuagint**
- **English Orthodox translations**
- **English NKJV**
- **Arabic Bible texts**
- **Arabic texts based on Coptic liturgical sources**
- **Coptic**
- **French**
- Additional aligned translations and traditions

The database is designed to preserve the differences between the Septuagint and Masoretic traditions rather than forcing every translation into an inaccurate one-to-one structure.

This is especially important in books where numbering or content differs substantially, including:

- Psalms
- Esther
- Daniel
- Jeremiah
- Job
- Proverbs
- 1 Samuel
- Other Old Testament books with textual differences

## Psalms

The Psalms receive special treatment within CHC because of their importance in both Scripture and Coptic worship.

CHC supports:

- Septuagint Psalm numbering
- Liturgical Psalm introductions
- Coptic-based English and Arabic texts
- Standard Arabic Bible text
- Greek
- Coptic
- Additional translations
- Alignment between different textual traditions

Introductions that belong to the liturgical text can be stored separately from numbered verses so that the original verse numbering remains correct.

## Multilingual Support

Coptic worship is inherently multilingual, and CHC is designed around that reality.

Content can be displayed in combinations of:

- Coptic
- English
- Arabic
- Greek
- French

The system is designed so translations remain aligned with the same prayer, hymn, verse, or liturgical unit whenever possible.

This makes CHC useful both for congregational worship and for users learning Coptic texts through another language.

## Hymns

The hymn system is built to organize Coptic hymns according to their actual liturgical use rather than treating them as an unstructured list of songs.

Hymns can be connected to:

- Services
- Seasons
- Feasts
- Saints
- Liturgical positions
- Day-of-week conditions
- Adam and Vatos structures
- Specific rites or occasions

The goal is to make it easy to find both **what is sung** and **when it is sung**.

## Synaxarion and Saints

CHC includes the Coptic Synaxarion and resources related to the saints of the Church.

The Synaxarion is organized according to the Coptic calendar and can contain both English and Arabic text.

This allows users to read the commemorations of each Coptic day alongside the rest of the liturgical content associated with that date.

Saints can also be used throughout the app to organize items such as:

- Doxologies
- Hymns of the Intercessions
- Gospel Responses
- Praxis Responses
- Verses of the Cymbals
- Veneration texts

A consistent saint order helps these sections remain organized across the application.

## Designed for Different Uses

CHC is intended to be useful in several settings.

### Personal Use

Users can read Scripture, learn hymns, follow prayers, listen to recordings, and explore the liturgical tradition from their own devices.

### Church Services

Deacons, cantors, servants, clergy, and congregations can use CHC to follow services and display liturgical text.

### Hymn Study

Students can view hymn texts, translations, seasonal placement, and recordings together.

### Sunday School and Teaching

Teachers and servants can quickly access Scripture, saints, liturgical texts, and related resources.

### Research and Preservation

Structured multilingual texts make it possible to preserve and compare Coptic liturgical and Scriptural traditions more carefully than would be possible with isolated documents.

---

## Design Philosophy

CHC is built around several principles.

### Preserve the Tradition

Technology should organize and present the rites of the Church without flattening their structure or removing meaningful differences between traditions.

### Keep the Interface Simple

The underlying liturgical rules can be complex, but the user should not need to understand the database in order to use the app.

### Keep Related Content Together

A hymn should be able to connect to its text, translation, recording, season, service, and liturgical position.

A Bible reading should connect naturally with the service in which it is read.

A saint should connect with the hymns and responses associated with that saint.

### Support the Entire Coptic Year

The application is built around the full cycle of ordinary days, fasts, feasts, seasons, saints, and special occasions.

### Work Across Devices

CHC is intended to provide a consistent experience across desktop, web, tablet, and mobile devices.

## Technology

The CHC application is built using a modern cross-platform architecture.

### React Native / Expo

The frontend is built with **React Native and Expo**, allowing CHC to share a large portion of its codebase across:

- iOS
- iPadOS
- Android
- Web

The application can still provide platform-specific behavior where necessary.

### Supabase

**Supabase** provides much of the structured backend used by CHC, including:

- Liturgical content
- Bible texts
- Hymns
- Saints
- Psalmody
- Seasonal conditions
- Metadata
- User-related data
- Database relationships

The database structure allows CHC to represent material as interconnected liturgical data rather than static pages.

### Cloudflare

**Cloudflare** is used within the wider CHC infrastructure for areas involving media delivery, storage, streaming, and web services.

This is particularly important as the audio side of CHC continues to expand.

## The CHC Ecosystem

The main Coptic Hymns Centre app is the center of a larger ecosystem.

### Coptic Hymns Centre

The main application used to read, listen, study, search, and follow Coptic Orthodox resources.

### CHC Artists

A companion platform for approved cantors, choirs, musicians, and publishers to submit and manage audio distributed through CHC.

Together, these applications allow CHC to support both the people using Coptic resources and the people helping create and preserve them.

## Project Goal

The long-term goal of Coptic Hymns Centre is to create a comprehensive digital home for Coptic Orthodox worship and tradition.

CHC aims to make the Church's Scripture, rites, hymns, saints, languages, and recordings easier to access without separating them from the liturgical context that gives them meaning.

The project is continually expanding as more texts, translations, recordings, books, and liturgical structures are added.
